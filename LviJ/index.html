Is firing modeled?

Yes: the field carries each visible player's attack and reload keys and weapon, and the generator reads them; zeroing them changes the shooter's region 1.71× as much as background, and muzzle flashes are drawn.

Generated (left) and the recording of the same view (right); red cells mark the frames with the attack key held.

Details

Firing and weapon are modeled: the field carries each visible player's attack and reload keys and its weapon (App. F); hits, damage and deaths are not determined by the model (App. G). The test below checks whether the generator reads these channels.

What latency does a player see?

About 0.73 s from key press to screen at the median, set by the 1-s block as in any block-wise generator: a press waits for the next block's control read, and the first frame follows in under 0.2 s.

Tiny decoder, two or four clients on H200, closed loop; segments 1 and 2 drawn to scale.

Details

Input-to-display latency is set by the block size, as in any block-wise generator: controls are read once per 1-s block, just before it is generated (App. B), so a key press waits for the next block's control read, 0.50 s at the median. Generating that block's first frame then takes under 0.2 s (172–175 ms with the tiny decoder), and its frames stream at 16 fps.

Segments 1 and 2 to scale, tiny decoder. The total is measured end to end and also includes the lockstep, network and display (table below). Timed on H200, one generator per GPU, two or four clients in lockstep on one machine, closed loop, scene state, 16-fps pacing; as in Table 7, other players' controls come from the recording.

Details

Measured on our cluster with NCCL: every layer's exchange is issued in order as a denoising step needs it, paced at the rate 16-fps video needs; the volumes are those Table 7 computes (reproduced to its printed precision). Time per step: the member minimum of the GPUs' own elapsed times, mean over steps; share of the frame budget = time per step × steps per frame / 62.5 ms. Transfer only: no model, no overlap of compute and communication. Between the two machines over TCP, Solaris finished within its slot for none of its paced steps at any P; every other row kept up in 98–100% of them. Gamma-World's volume is its hub's partial attention results, not raw keys and values; WorldCast's is one 12-kB message per other client and one 387-kB memory entry per 1-s block.
