What latency does a player see?

About 0.73 s from key press to screen at the median, set by the 1-s block as in any block-wise generator: a press waits for the next block's control read, and the first frame follows in under 0.2 s.

Tiny decoder, two or four clients on H200, closed loop; segments 1 and 2 drawn to scale.

Details

Input-to-display latency is set by the block size, as in any block-wise generator: controls are read once per 1-s block, just before it is generated (App. B), so a key press waits for the next block's control read, 0.50 s at the median. Generating that block's first frame then takes under 0.2 s (172–175 ms with the tiny decoder), and its frames stream at 16 fps.

Details

Rates differ in four ways: the statistic (own compute, barrier to barrier with waits, or the paced generation rate), the decoder (full VAE on the same GPU or a second one, or the tiny decoder), the code path (evaluation harness, or the serving path as released or optimized) and the GPU (H200, H20, L20). H20 runs at 10 and 16 clients span two machines (8 + 2, 8 + 8).

What each optimization does

  • Warm-up and steady scheduling: the session is warmed up before timing, and each client is pinned to its CPUs.
  • Message mesh: clients exchange messages over direct connections instead of polling a shared directory; a waiting client wakes as soon as a message arrives.
  • Shared depth: a client's depth map travels with its memory entry, so peers do not recompute it.
  • Retrieval threads: memory retrieval projects the cameras on four threads, with the matrix products under one lock so that results stay identical.
  • Tile-limited retrieval: only the image tiles that can fill a still-missing pixel are projected.
  • Background reprojection: a client's own reprojections run on a background thread.
  • Peer cache: received latents are cached instead of re-read.
  • Early hand-off: a block's frames go to the decoder before the block's bookkeeping finishes.
  • Prefetch: GPU work that needs only the client's own data starts right after the block is committed.
  • Every optimization is off by default; with them on, each client's latents stay identical to the offline client's.

Details

Measured on our cluster with NCCL: every layer's exchange is issued in order as a denoising step needs it, paced at the rate 16-fps video needs; the volumes are those Table 7 computes (reproduced to its printed precision). Time per step: the member minimum of the GPUs' own elapsed times, mean over steps; share of the frame budget = time per step × steps per frame / 62.5 ms. Transfer only: no model, no overlap of compute and communication. Between the two machines over TCP, Solaris finished within its slot for none of its paced steps at any P; every other row kept up in 98–100% of them. Gamma-World's volume is its hub's partial attention results, not raw keys and values; WorldCast's is one 12-kB message per other client and one 387-kB memory entry per 1-s block.
